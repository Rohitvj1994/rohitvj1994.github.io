    WHO Comparison

    The WHO 2021 guidelines are health-based reference levels. Each one belongs to a specific averaging period.

    Short term: against 24-hour levels

    Long term: if this level lasted a whole year

    Read this as a reference, not a safe/unsafe verdict. The WHO 24-hour level is a 24-hour mean that may be exceeded on 3–4 days a year (99th percentile). A reading taken at one moment, or a PM2.5 value inferred from AQI, is not the same quantity. CPCB's AQI uses 24-hour averages, so the comparison is closest when your number is a 24-hour figure. WHO found no threshold below which PM2.5 is harmless, so lower is better at every level.

    What does this pollution mean for your body?

    Lungs and airways

    Fine particles irritate the lining of the airways. On polluted days people report coughing, wheezing, a tight chest and shortness of breath, and asthma attacks become more frequent. Lung function can dip for hours to days. Years of exposure are linked with slower lung growth in children, chronic bronchitis, COPD and lung cancer.
